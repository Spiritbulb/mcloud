Selling online in Kenya no longer needs a developer or a big budget. You need four things: something to sell, a website customers trust, a way to take M-PESA, and a plan for delivery. This guide works through each one in order, with the settings to use on Menengai Cloud.

## 1. Decide what you're selling, and to whom

Before building anything, write down:

- **Your products.** Physical goods to deliver, or services you perform? Menengai Cloud handles both. Products have stock and variants, and [Services](/docs/services) are for work you do for customers.
- **Your customers.** Nairobi only, or all of Kenya? This decides your delivery set-up and your prices.
- **Your prices, including delivery.** Kenyan shoppers compare prices quickly. Work out what each order really costs you, packaging and courier included.

Start small. Ten products with good photos and clear prices sell better than a hundred half-finished listings.

## 2. Set up your store

Create a free site, then follow the order in the [Overview](/docs/overview#overview-first):

1. **General settings.** Set your site name, a short description and your currency (KES unless you sell abroad). See [General](/docs/general).
2. **Look and feel.** Open the [Editor](/docs/editor) to set your colours and fonts and edit each section of your pages. A clean, consistent look builds trust.
3. **Add your products.** For each one, add a clear name, a description that answers buyers' questions, the price and good photos. Turn on inventory tracking if you have limited stock, and use variants for sizes and colours. See [Products](/docs/products#products-adding).

> Write product names the way customers search. "Women's leather sandals, size 37–41" is easier to find than "Summer Collection Item 4".

## 3. Take M-PESA payments

M-PESA is how most Kenyans pay, so set it up before you launch. You have two options:

- **Manual M-PESA.** Customers pay your till or paybill and enter the code. It works on every plan.
- **STK push.** Customers get a prompt on their phone and orders mark themselves paid. It needs the Hobby plan and a Safaricom Daraja app.

Either way, the money goes straight to your own account. The [M-PESA payments guide](/docs/guides/accept-mpesa-payments-on-your-website) walks through both.

## 4. Plan your delivery

Delivery is where many Kenyan online shops win or lose customers. In [Delivery options](/docs/delivery):

1. **Add your couriers.** For example, a rider for Nairobi and a courier company for upcountry. If the courier has a tracking page, add its tracking URL so customers can follow their parcel.
2. **Add delivery zones with a rate for each**, such as *Nairobi CBD*, *Westlands* or *Mombasa*. Enter 0 for free delivery.

Customers outside your zones aren't shown a delivery option at checkout and are pointed to your contact page instead. Once a courier collects an order, add the tracking number on [Track orders](/docs/tracking).

## 5. Go live and get your first orders

1. Turn on **Site active** in General so visitors can see your store.
2. Place a test order yourself, all the way to payment.
3. Share your store link on WhatsApp Status, Instagram, TikTok and Facebook, with a direct link to a product, not just your home page.
4. Ask your first customers for a photo or a review, and post it.

When you're ready, connect your own domain such as *yourshop.co.ke*. It looks more professional and is easier to remember. See [using your own domain](/docs/guides/custom-domain-for-your-business-website).

## What it costs

You can start on the **Free** plan for KES 0, with up to 20 products and 50 orders a month. **Hobby** (KES 795 a month) raises that to 200 products and 500 orders, and adds a custom domain, a blog, detailed analytics and STK push. **Pro** (KES 2,795 a month) removes the limits and Menengai Cloud branding. Plans are paid from your organisation wallet by M-PESA. See [Plans and billing](/docs/billing).
